## Methods and Thresholds

- `detect_outliers_iqr(series, k=1.5)` flags values outside the conventional Tukey fences. It is robust to non-normality but can mark valid tail observations.
- `detect_outliers_zscore(series, threshold=3.0)` uses the absolute population z-score (`ddof=0`), matching the fact that the notebook analyzes the full generated sample rather than estimating a sample standard deviation for inference. It is sensitive to skew and heavy tails.
- `winsorize_series(series, lower=0.05, upper=0.95)` clips both tails at the 5th and 95th empirical percentiles. This retains row count while changing extreme magnitudes.

All functions validate numeric, non-empty, finite inputs and parameter ranges. Missing values are excluded from threshold estimation, remain unflagged by detectors, and remain missing after winsorization.

## Sensitivity Design

The notebook analyzes `daily_return` and creates both IQR and z-score boolean flags. It then compares:

1. **All observations:** no treatment.
2. **IQR-filtered:** rows flagged with `k=1.5` are excluded.
3. **Winsorized:** both regression variables are clipped at their own 5th/95th percentiles.

For each variant, it reports mean, median, standard deviation, observations, regression slope/intercept, in-sample R², and MAE. Metrics are diagnostic sensitivity checks, not evidence that the treatment with the highest in-sample score is universally best.

## Main Findings

- The IQR rule flags nine of 115 observations, including all five injected shocks and four less-extreme tail returns. The 3-sigma rule flags exactly the five injected shocks.
- The all-data regression has a slope near the generating relationship of 0.6 and very high R² because both return columns share the injected shocks.
- Removing IQR observations lowers R² substantially even though MAE changes only modestly. This demonstrates that the extreme events carry genuine shared signal in this constructed dataset.
- Winsorizing both variables produces an intermediate fit and the lowest treatment-specific MAE, but it changes the response values themselves; its metric cannot be interpreted as an untouched-data forecast score.

## Risk Assumptions

- IQR fences assume quartiles represent the ordinary regime; z-scores assume a roughly symmetric, light-tailed distribution. Financial returns often violate both assumptions.
- Filtering may remove true stress events and make a volatility-risk model look safer than reality. For the Stage 01 project, flagged SPY extremes should normally be retained, studied by regime, and potentially used as the target rather than deleted.
- Winsorizing reduces influence without deleting dates, but it understates loss magnitude and can damage tail-risk estimates, expected shortfall, calibration, and stress testing.
- The simple regression is contemporaneous, seeded, and in-sample. It is a sensitivity demonstration, not a causal model or a valid next-day forecast.
- Thresholds must be estimated from training data only in later modeling. Recomputing them with validation/test periods would leak future distribution information.
- Different outlier rules answer different questions. Disagreement between IQR and z-score flags is documented rather than resolved by choosing whichever result looks best.
